# Analysis plan (written before any detector was run on real data)

Status: frozen for MIT-BIH AFDB development on 2026-11-20 (git commit that adds
this file). The LTAFDB analysis runs only after the AFDB analysis is complete,
with the settings below unchanged. Any change after this point is logged at the
end of this file with its reason and date.

## Data

* MIT-BIH Atrial Fibrillation Database (AFDB) 1.0.0, all 25 records. Reference:
  `.atr` rhythm annotations. Detector input: `.qrs` unaudited beat annotations.
  Records 00735 and 03665 have no signal files and no record length in the header.
* Long Term AF Database (LTAFDB) 1.0.0, all 84 records. Reference: rhythm
  annotations in `.atr`. Detector input: `.qrs` unaudited beat annotations
  (generated by `sqrs`, per the database's ANNOTATORS file).
* Both databases: one record per subject according to their documentation, so
  records are treated as independent subjects. This is an assumption taken from
  the documentation and is stated wherever bootstrap intervals are shown.

## Target period and reference states

* Target period: `[0, n_samples / fs)` from the header. For records whose
  header gives no length (AFDB 00735, 03665): `[0, (last annotation sample + 1) / fs)`
  over `.atr` and `.qrs`, labelled "length declared from annotations".
* Event: rhythm label `(AFIB`. Non-event: every other rhythm label, including
  `(AFL` (the EC38:2007 convention used by `epicmp`).
* Time before the first rhythm annotation: unknown reference
  (`(unlabelled)`), excluded from every reference-known denominator.
* Sensitivity analyses: (S1) `(AFL` counted as event; (S2) time before the first
  rhythm annotation counted as `(N`.

## Detector (one detector, two operating policies)

RR-irregularity rule with the statistics and thresholds of Dash et al. (2009,
Table 1): a 128-beat RR segment is AF when RMSSD > 0.1 x mean RR, AND
0.54 < TPR < 0.77, AND SE > 0.7, with SE computed after removing the 8 longest
and 8 shortest intervals, using 16 equal-width bins. The ectopic-beat filter of
the original paper is not implemented. Thresholds were selected by the original
authors on MIT-BIH AFDB, so AFDB results are not independent of threshold
selection. No thresholds were tuned here.

* Input: unaudited beat times only. No reference rhythm, no reference-derived
  quality flag, no future beats.
* A decision is issued at every 16th beat once 128 RR intervals are available,
  using the 128 intervals that end at that beat (real-time, causal).
* Each decision holds from its issue time until the next decision, for at most
  10 s. Uncovered time between the first and last decision becomes `abstain`
  with reason "no recent beats". Time before the first decision becomes
  `abstain` with reason "warm-up (fewer than 128 RR intervals)".
* Observation: input is `available` from the first to the last beat annotation
  and `unavailable` outside that span (reason "beat annotation stream not started"
  / "beat annotation stream ended"). No other input gaps are inferred.
* Policy A ("answer when possible"): as above.
* Policy B ("quality-gated"): as A, but a decision is replaced by `abstain`
  (reason "implausible RR intervals") when more than 5% of the 128 RR
  intervals lie outside [0.3, 2.0] s. This is a beat-series plausibility gate,
  not a signal-quality index. A and B are two policies of one detector.

## Matching and outcome definitions

* Primary matching: one-to-one, positive overlap (> 0 s), scope `"full"`,
  eligible episodes have at least 30 s of reference-known AF time, no learning
  period, no merging of episodes or alarms, no minimum alarm duration.
* Secondary: `any_overlap` (epicmp rule) with the same eligibility.
* Primary outcomes: full-period AF capture; false alarms per reference-known
  hour; answer coverage.
* Secondary outcomes: episode sensitivity; burden difference on answered time
  (percentage points) and burden error (minutes).
* Every outcome is reported pooled (ratio of sums) and as an equal-record mean.
* Uncertainty: subject-level paired bootstrap, B = 2000, seed 20261120,
  percentile 95% intervals; undefined replicates are counted.
* Comparison of A and B: own answered time, shared answered time, and full
  period, with discarded time and episodes.

## Reference comparison with WFDB epicmp

* For every AFDB record and both policies, the detector output is written as a
  WFDB test annotation file (positive = `(AFIB`, anything else = `(N`), and
  `epicmp 10.7.0 -A -` is run with default options. Its counts and durations are
  compared with `wfdb_epicmp_af()` on the same files. The same is done for
  LTAFDB after the freeze.

## Pre-specified audit exhibit

* The record with the largest reference-unknown duration in each database, and
  the AFDB record with the largest difference between Policy A's and Policy B's
  answered time. These are chosen by these structural rules, not by results.

## Controlled experiments (simulated, labelled as such)

* AFDB, Policy A: (i) random outages, (ii) event-concentrated outages
  (artificial stress test). 6 outages of 20 minutes per record, seeds 1 to 20.
